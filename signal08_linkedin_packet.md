# LinkedIn post packet — Gokul's Data Signal 08

## Caption

AI strategy is becoming an infrastructure strategy.

The IEA’s April 2026 update projects global data-centre electricity use rising from 485 TWh in 2025 to about 950 TWh in 2030—almost doubling in five years. The more useful leadership question isn’t whether AI demand will grow. It’s whether power, grid access and resilience can scale with it.

Three signals stood out to me:

• AI-focused data-centre electricity use is projected to triple by 2030.  
• AI server power density rose 11× from 2020 to 2025 and could rise another 4× by 2027.  
• Data centres could drive almost half of US electricity-demand growth to 2030—and more than half in Japan.

For data and technology leaders, capacity planning now needs to connect model demand, location, power availability, cooling and business continuity. A roadmap that ignores those dependencies can look ambitious in a slide deck but become constrained in delivery.

## Alt text

Executive infographic titled “AI’s next bottleneck isn’t compute. It’s power.” It shows global data-centre electricity consumption rising from 485 TWh in 2025 to 950 TWh in 2030, a 96% increase and 14.4% annual growth. Supporting panels show AI-focused data-centre electricity demand tripling by 2030, AI server power density increasing from an index of 1 in 2020 to 11 in 2025 and a projected 44 in 2027, and the estimated share of electricity-demand growth driven by data centres in the United States, Japan and Malaysia. Source: IEA, Key Questions on Energy and AI, April 2026.
